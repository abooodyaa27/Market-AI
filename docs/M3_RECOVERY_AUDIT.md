# M3 recovery, 22 September 2026

The screen showed GAP_M3 despite a fresh XAUUSD price. A direct BiQuote OHLC capture from the same day (20:02 UTC) returned 301 M1 bars, with missing minutes after 15:54, 16:14, and 16:19 UTC. Strict M1→M3 aggregation correctly dropped incomplete M3 buckets; the earlier snapshot rejected *all* of the most recent 80 M3 candles if any historical gap was still inside that window. In the capture, 60+ complete consecutive candles followed the last incomplete bucket, enough for the original 55-bar warmup.

The feature extractor now restarts only its per-timeframe indicator window immediately after a bad gap and requires at least 55 complete consecutive bars. It never inserts missing OHLC or marks an unobserved candle complete. A newer gap with fewer than 55 bars still blocks the frame and reports the last gap boundary and `contiguousBars / requiredBars` to the UI. Scheduled gold session gaps retain the existing separate validation rule; BTC has no such exception.

Replaying the captured four native OHLC feeds through the actual normalization, M1→M3 and M5→M15 aggregation, and six-frame snapshot reveals a separate `GAP_H1`: the source omitted the 20:00 UTC H1 bar on 21 September, although it reported other 20:00 M5 bars and some M15 bars. Its 15m feed also omitted 20:45 and its 5m feed omitted 20:55, so a complete H1 bar cannot be reconstructed from the available candles. The 20:00 interval included open-market minutes, and there are only 22 later complete H1 bars, below the 55-bar indicator warmup. The application therefore reports H1's true remaining reason and continues WAIT until enough trustworthy data arrive. It does not treat this provider outage as a normal gold session break or invent prices. This is a data-source limit, not a model-promotion change.

Tests cover gold/BTC recovery after 60 real bars, refusal after 20, the exact 54/55 boundary, multiple gaps, and the still-blocked H1 case.
